ABSTRACT

 

 

UrbanEye is a full-stack civic complaint and issue-resolution platform developed to improve the way public infrastructure problems are reported, analyzed, and tracked. In many existing complaint systems, citizens are able to submit issues, but they often receive little clarity about how the complaint is categorized, where it is routed, and whether any real action is being taken. This project addresses that limitation by transforming complaint submission from a one-time reporting activity into a structured and trackable workflow.

 

The platform integrates a web application, a mobile application, a backend API, and a FastAPI-based AI service to support end-to-end complaint handling. A user can report an issue by entering a title, description, location, and optional image data. The AI layer then enriches the complaint by identifying the likely category, estimating priority, suggesting the responsible department, generating a recommended action, and drafting a concise X-ready public update. This makes complaint records more meaningful and useful for both citizens and administrative authorities.

 

UrbanEye also provides dashboard-based tracking, complaint status visibility, and location-oriented monitoring features. The mobile application is designed to help residents easily check whether a complaint is pending, in progress, or resolved, while the web application focuses on broader operational visibility through summaries, complaint views, and administrative monitoring tools. The system also supports fallback behavior for local development and demonstration, making it resilient even when some external services are unavailable.

 

Overall, UrbanEye demonstrates how AI can be applied in a practical civic-tech environment to improve transparency, accountability, and structured decision-making in public grievance systems. The project serves as a scalable and extensible foundation for future enhancements such as direct social media publishing, image-based issue detection, multilingual complaint handling, and deeper integration with smart city governance workflows.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

 

 

UrbanEye is a full-stack civic complaint and issue-resolution platform designed to improve the way public issues are reported, analyzed, and tracked. The project was developed to address a common problem in existing grievance systems: citizens can submit complaints, but they often do not know what happens after submission, which department is responsible, or whether any action has started. UrbanEye solves this by turning complaint registration into a structured, transparent, and trackable workflow.

 

The platform combines a web application, a mobile application, a backend API, and a FastAPI-based AI service into one integrated system. Instead of storing a complaint only as plain text, UrbanEye enriches it with structured information such as category, priority, likely department, suggested action, and an X-ready public update. This makes complaint records more useful for both citizens and administrators.

UrbanEye also brings together complaint reporting, AI-assisted triage, dashboard-based monitoring, status visibility, and local fallback support in one ecosystem. Built using modern technologies such as Next.js, React, TypeScript, Node.js, Express.js, Prisma, FastAPI, React Native, and Expo, the project demonstrates modular design, practical AI integration, and deployment readiness suitable for civic-tech applications.

                  CHAPTER 2: PROBLEM STATEMENT

 

What Problem Does UrbanEye  Solve?
Core Problem: Existing civic complaint and grievance systems often fail to provide a clear, structured, and transparent process for reporting and tracking public issues. Citizens may submit complaints about problems such as garbage overflow, water leakage, broken streetlights, potholes, or drainage blockage, but they often do not know which department is responsible, how urgent the issue is, or whether any action has been taken. At the same time, authorities frequently receive complaints in unstructured form, making classification, prioritization, and monitoring more difficult.

Business Context: With increasing urbanization and growing dependence on digital governance, cities are receiving a higher volume of civic complaints from residents. However, many existing systems are fragmented, slow, and difficult to follow from the citizen’s point of view. People are often forced to rely on disconnected channels such as apps, forms, calls, or social media posts, which creates duplication, weak tracking, and low transparency. UrbanEye addresses this gap by bringing complaint submission, AI-assisted triage, structured status tracking, dashboard visibility, and public-update support into one integrated platform. This helps improve citizen trust, administrative clarity, and overall efficiency in public grievance handling.

 

 

2.1 : Problem Definition

Problem 1: Fragmentation of Complaint Reporting and Tracking Tools

 

Current State (Problem)

In the current civic complaint environment, citizens often depend on multiple disconnected channels to report, follow up, and escalate public issues. This creates a fragmented and inefficient experience for both citizens and authorities.

 

Existing Fragmented Civic Complaint Ecosystem

In many cities, complaint reporting is not handled through one clear and unified system. Instead, users may rely on different platforms and communication channels for different parts of the complaint process. Because these channels are disconnected, complaints are often difficult to track, compare, or manage properly.

 

Typical Workflow of a Citizen

A resident dealing with a civic issue often has to move through several separate methods before expecting any action:

 

Online Complaint Portals

 

● Submit issue details through government or municipal web forms
● Fill long forms with limited guidance
● Often receive little clarity after submission
● Mobile Apps or Local Service Apps

 

● Report issues through app-based complaint sections
● Access only basic status updates
● Limited complaint analysis or structured categorization
● Phone Calls and Helplines
 
● Contact departments manually for follow-up
● Repeat complaint details multiple times
● No reliable digital tracking trail
● Social Media Platforms

 

● Post issues publicly for faster attention
● Tag authorities manually
● No proper integration with official complaint records
● Personal Notes or Manual Tracking

 

● Keep screenshots, complaint IDs, or written notes
● Track complaint progress manually
● No centralized record of all filed issues
● Resulting Problems
● Because the process is spread across multiple disconnected channels

 

● citizens face confusion and repetition
● complaint status remains unclear
● authorities receive unstructured information
● prioritization becomes inconsistent
● tracking and accountability become weaker

 

 

 

Challenges and Limitations

This fragmented complaint workflow leads to several critical issues:

 

● High Follow-Up Burden
● Citizens often have to move between portals, apps, calls, and social media to report and follow up on the same issue. This increases effort and makes the process frustrating.

 

● Lack of Status Clarity
● After submitting a complaint, users often do not know whether it has been received, reviewed, assigned, or resolved. This weakens trust in the system.

 

● Unstructured Complaint Records
● Complaints are frequently submitted in free-form text without consistent category, priority, or department mapping. This makes interpretation and routing harder for authorities.

 

● Disconnected Reporting and Escalation
● Complaint submission and public escalation often happen on separate platforms. A user may file a complaint officially and then separately post about it on social media, creating duplication and inconsistency.

 

● Inefficient Decision-Making
● Without a unified view of complaint data, authorities may struggle to identify urgent issues, repeated complaints, or location-based patterns. This can delay action.

 

● Poor Administrative Visibility
● When complaint data is scattered across systems, dashboards and summaries become less reliable. This reduces the ability to monitor performance and plan responses effectively.

 

● Suboptimal User Experience
● The overall complaint-handling process becomes slow, repetitive, and difficult to understand. Instead of helping the citizen, the system often adds confusion.

 

 

 

 

Problem 2:

Disconnected Complaint Reporting from Resolution Visibility

Current State (Problem)

 

In many existing systems, complaint submission and complaint tracking exist as separate experiences. A citizen may be able to file a complaint, but the later stages such as classification, routing, action, and resolution are often unclear or poorly connected.

 

Phase 1: Complaint Submission – Basic but Incomplete

At the first stage, the user is able to report a civic problem through a form, app, or local portal. However, this stage usually ends after the complaint is submitted.

 

A user may:

 

● enter the complaint title and description.
● provide a location.
● upload a photo if supported.
● receive a complaint number or confirmation.

 

Key Limitation:

 

● submission happens, but there is no clear understanding of what happens next.
● no structured explanation of category, department, or urgency.
● limited visibility into how the complaint enters the action pipeline.

 

 

Phase 2:

Post-Submission Follow-Up – Confusing and Uncertain

After the complaint is filed, the user often enters a second phase where visibility becomes weak. Even though the issue is recorded, the user may not know whether it is under review, assigned, delayed, or solved.

 

The citizen may:

 

● check the status repeatedly.
● contact departments manually.
● use social media for escalation.
● depend on screenshots or complaint IDs for follow-up.
● receive unclear or delayed responses.

● low trust in the complaint system.
● frustration due to uncertainty.
● repeated reporting of the same issue.
● weak sense of accountability.

 

 

 

Root Causes

Submission-Tracking Gap

 

Complaint filing happens in one step, while actual resolution visibility is scattered or incomplete. There is no strong bridge between submitting the issue and understanding its progress.No Structured Complaint Journey.

 

Users are rarely shown a clear complaint lifecycle such as:

 

● complaint received
● complaint categorized
● complaint assigned
● complaint in progress
● complaint resolved

 

Because of this, users do not understand where their complaint stands. No Integrated Interpretation Layer.

Without these fields, complaints are harder to route and monitor.

const http = require('http');

function makeRequest(path, method = 'GET', data = null) {

 return new Promise((resolve, reject) => {

   const options = { hostname: 'localhost', port: 5000, path, method, headers: { 'Content-Type': 'application/json' } };

   const req = http.request(options, (res) => {

     let body = '';

     res.on('data', (chunk) => { body += chunk; });

     res.on('end', () => resolve({ statusCode: res.statusCode, body }));

   });

   req.on('error', reject);

   if (data) req.write(JSON.stringify(data));

   req.end();

 });

}
